Best Dog Parks in San Diego, CA

An amenity-first look at City of San Diego approved leash-free locations — fencing, water, small-dog pens, hours, and beach/island options.

The City of San Diego Park & Recreation Department publishes an official Approved Leash Free Locations list with more than 20 off-leash dog areas citywide — from enclosed neighborhood pens to Dog Beach in Ocean Beach and Fiesta Island in Mission Bay.

Outside designated off-leash zones, dogs must stay on a leash not exceeding 8 feet in city parks (including trails and canyons). Owners enter leash-free areas at their own risk and must clean up waste. Hours vary widely: some sites are dawn–dusk, others 24 hours, and a few post weekend-only or special windows (for example Grape Street).

Below we highlight 12 well-documented locations from the city’s leash-free table with amenity fields taken from that page. Unknowns are labeled — see sources.md. Additional city sites (Cadman, Centrum, Charles L. Lewis III, Dusty Rhodes, Gompers, Grape Street, Rancho Bernardo, Torrey Highlands, and others) appear on the official list but are not expanded here yet.

Leash rules outside the zone San Diego requires dogs leashed until you are inside the posted off-leash boundary. Beach and Mission Bay timing rules can differ from inland parks — check sandiego.gov dogs in parks before coastal visits.

Dog Beach (Ocean Beach) Swim

Foot of Voltaire Street · Ocean Beach

  • Fenced No — open beach / ocean access
  • Water Yes — beach and ocean swim
  • Small-dog area No dedicated small-dog section
  • Hours 24 hours (city leash-free table)

Classic San Diego off-leash beach. Strong recall and surf awareness required; clean up waste; leash on access paths as posted.

Fiesta Island Open

  • Small-dog area No
  • Hours 6 a.m. – 10 p.m. (city leash-free table)

Large Mission Bay off-leash destination. Vehicles and open space — keep dogs visible and under voice control; bring drinking water.

FAQ — San Diego dog parks

Do dogs need to be on a leash in San Diego parks?

Yes, except inside a posted designated dog off-leash area. City guidance: leash not to exceed 8 feet in parks, trails, and canyons, and leash until you are inside the off-leash boundary.

What are typical hours?

It depends on the site. Examples from the city table: Dog Beach and Capehart are listed 24 hours; Fiesta Island is 6 a.m.–10 p.m.; Kearny Mesa is 6:30 a.m.–10 p.m.; many neighborhood pens are dawn to dusk. Always read posted signs.

Where can my dog swim?

Dog Beach at the foot of Voltaire Street in Ocean Beach is the city’s named beach/ocean off-leash swim spot. Fiesta Island offers Mission Bay shoreline in places — confirm current posted boundaries. Bring fresh drinking water either way.

Are there separate small-dog areas?

Several enclosed parks list dual pens, including Capehart, Doyle, Nobel, Ward Canyon, Rancho Peñasquitos, and Gompers (on the city list). Open beach/island sites generally do not separate by size.

What should I bring?

Leash (required outside the zone), waste bags, fresh water and a bowl, and sun/heat plans for midday. Coastal parks can still be hot on asphalt paths (Fiesta Island). Tick/flea prevention matters year-round in SoCal grass and brush.
