Best Dog Parks in Sacramento, CA

An amenity-first look at City of Sacramento off-leash dog parks — fencing, water, small-dog space, posted rules, and what the city pages actually list.

The City of Sacramento Department of Youth, Parks, & Community Enrichment (YPCE) publishes an Off-Leash Dog Parks list with 15 named parks. The city’s Specialty Parks page currently says Sacramento is home to 14 off-leash dog parks. This hub covers every park named on the dedicated dog-parks list.

YPCE states that all of its dog parks are fenced, with a double-gate system for safety, and that parks include benches, shade areas, and drinking fountains for dogs. We mark water and small-dog areas Yes only where a park page or the list’s per-park notes specifically mention them; otherwise they stay Unknown. Posted hours are not listed on the city dog-parks page.

Outside those fenced runs, city park guidance says dogs must be on leashes. Dog-park rules include: a current license on the dog’s collar; off-leash only inside the designated fenced area; clean up after your dog; at least one handler for every two dogs; children under 14 accompanied by an adult; no female dogs in season; and immediate removal of aggressive or unruly dogs. Owners use the parks at their own risk.

FAQ — Sacramento dog parks

What is the best dog park in Sacramento?

For listed amenities, North Natomas Regional Park is the most detailed city page — drinking fountains, dog-wash faucets, a fire-hydrant spray feature, agility equipment, and shade canopies, plus separate large and small yards. Sutter’s Landing’s dog park is 2.5 acres with large- and small-dog areas near Midtown. Valley Oak combines a 2-acre large-dog yard and a half-acre small-dog yard with dog drinking fountains. Truitt Bark Park is the compact Midtown option with separate fenced yards.

Which Sacramento dog parks have a small-dog area?

Official pages or list notes name separate small-dog areas at Blackbird, Glenbrook, Lynn Robie, North Natomas Regional Park, Regency Community Park, Sutter’s Landing, Truitt Bark Park, and Valley Oak. The other listed parks do not mention a separate small-dog yard.

Are Sacramento dog parks fenced?

Yes. The city’s Off-Leash Dog Parks page says all YPCE dog parks are fenced with a double-gate system. Dogs may be off leash only inside the designated fenced area.

Do I need a Sacramento dog license?

Yes for the parks: the posted dog-park rules require a current license on the dog’s collar. The city also runs a pet-licensing program (an unlicensed dog can draw a correctable citation). Front Street Animal Shelter’s licensing partner sends renewal reminders.

What are the hours?

The official Off-Leash Dog Parks page does not list opening or closing times. Check posted signs at the gate, or contact the city, before you go. Jacinto Creek Park’s page says its restroom is open 24/7 — that is the restroom, not a stated dog-park hour.

What should I bring?

A leash (off-leash only inside the fenced run), waste bags (you must clean up), current license tags on the collar, and fresh water with a bowl — only some park pages specifically list dog drinking water. Central Valley summers are hot, so go early or late.
