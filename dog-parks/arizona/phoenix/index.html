Best Dog Parks in Phoenix, AZ

An amenity-first look at all 13 City of Phoenix off-leash dog parks — shade, water, restrooms, small-dog space, and the long hours that make desert dog parks usable.

City of Phoenix Parks and Recreation runs 13 off-leash dog parks, listed on its Dog Parks page and in the city’s parks open-data layer. The city says all of them are fenced with safe, double-gated entrances.

In Phoenix, shade and water decide whether a dog park is usable from May to September, so this hub calls them out for every park. Amenities come from each park’s city page; where a page doesn’t list something, we mark it Unknown. Only Steele Indian School’s page names separate small and large dog parks.

Hours are long: eight parks open at 5:30 a.m., and six of those stay open until 11 p.m., which gives you cool early-morning and late-evening windows. Rose Mofford closes every Thursday for maintenance.

Rules from the city: leash on until you are inside with the gates fully closed (the Phoenix leash law applies everywhere else), dogs must be licensed in Maricopa County, older than 4 months, and currently vaccinated, no female dogs in heat, no smoking, alcohol, or food inside the fences, no grooming, and no children under 12. Mutt-mitt dispensers and bins sit along the fence lines.

Summer turf closures Phoenix closes dog parks in rotation during the summer for turf recovery and maintenance (for example, Steele Indian School’s yards July 6 through August 17, and Hance Park every May to June). Check the 2026 summer closure list before you drive.

FAQ — Phoenix dog parks

What is the best dog park in Phoenix?

For listed amenities, Steele Indian School Dog Park is the standout: it is the only city page that names separate small and large dog parks, and it lists shade, water, restrooms, and ADA access. Downtown, Margaret T. Hance Dog Park lists the same core amenities. For late visits, Pinnacle, Deer Valley, Deem Hills, Esteban, Aguila, and RJ Dog Park at Pecos Park are open until 11 p.m.

Which Phoenix dog parks have shade?

City pages list shade structures at 11 of the 13 parks: every park except Grovers Basin and Thelda Williams Paw Pup Park, whose pages do not list shade.

Which Phoenix dog parks have a small-dog area?

Steele Indian School is the only park whose city page names separate small and large dog parks. The other pages do not mention a separate small-dog yard, so we list them as Unknown.

What are Phoenix dog park hours?

It varies by park. Pinnacle, Deer Valley, Deem Hills, Esteban, Aguila, and RJ at Pecos open 5:30 a.m.–11 p.m.; Grovers Basin and Paradise Valley 5:30 a.m.–10 p.m.; Steele Indian School 6 a.m.–10 p.m.; Rose Mofford 6 a.m.–10 p.m. but closed Thursdays; Washington 6:30 a.m.–10 p.m.; Hance 6:30 a.m.–9 p.m.; and Paw Pup Park 7 a.m.–8 p.m.

Do I need a dog license to use Phoenix dog parks?

Yes. The city’s rules note that Maricopa County requires all dogs in the county to be licensed, and dogs at the parks must be older than 4 months and currently vaccinated.

What should I bring?

A leash for the walk in, waste bags (dispensers are along the fence lines but can run out), and fresh water with a bowl, especially from May to September. Go early or late in summer, when pavement and turf can get dangerously hot for paws.
